## The Setup: Not Your Cousin's Supergirl

You know the story. Superman is the beacon of hope. He's the big blue Boy Scout. He's the symbol of truth, justice, and the American way.

His cousin, Kara Zor-El, is none of those things. She's angry. She's traumatised. She's spent years hiding on Earth, suppressing her powers, trying to forget the destruction of her home planet. She's not a hero. She's a survivor.

*Supergirl* doesn't try to be *Superman*. It doesn't try to replicate the hopeful, aspirational tone of the classic Superman films. It's darker. It's grungier. It's punk-rock. And it's all the better for it.

The film opens with Kara (Milly Alcock) living a quiet life on Earth. She's working a dead-end job. She's trying to blend in. She's trying to forget. But her past catches up with her when a ruthless bounty hunter named Krem (Matthias Schoenaerts) arrives on Earth, hunting for a weapon that Kara's father hid years ago. Kara is forced to confront her past, her powers, and her destiny.

What follows is a film that is part coming-of-age story, part revenge thriller, and part superhero origin story. It's not perfect. But it's a refreshingly different take on a character who has never really been given her due on the big screen.

---

## The Acting: Milly Alcock Is a Revelation

**Milly Alcock** is the reason to watch this film. She's magnetic. She's raw. She's vulnerable. She's angry. She's everything that Supergirl should be. Her Kara is not a symbol. She's a person. She's a young woman who has lost everything, who is trying to find her place in the world, who is trying to figure out who she is.

Alcock brings a physicality to the role that is genuinely impressive. She does her own stunts. She throws punches with real weight. She moves with the confidence of someone who has been trained to fight. But she also brings a quiet sadness to Kara that makes the character deeply sympathetic.

**Jason Momoa** as Lobo is a scene-stealer. He's chaotic, he's violent, and he's clearly having the time of his life. He's the perfect foil for Kara — a man who has no regrets, no guilt, no trauma. He just wants to fight, drink, and get paid. Momoa's Lobo is a welcome addition to the DC universe, and it's clear that he's having fun with the role.

**Matthias Schoenaerts** as Krem is the film's weak link. He's not a bad actor, but he's given nothing to work with. Krem is a generic villain with a generic motivation. He's evil because the plot requires him to be evil. He's not memorable. He's not threatening. He's just there.

---

## The Verdict: A Worthy Addition to the DC Universe

*Supergirl* is not a masterpiece. The villain is weak. The middle act drags. Some of the effects are uneven. But it's a film that understands its protagonist. It gives Kara a story that matters — a story about grief, identity, and finding the courage to be who you are.

It's not the Supergirl you remember from the comics. It's not the Supergirl from the TV show. It's something new. Something different. Something that might not appeal to everyone. But it's a film that deserves a chance.

**Score: 7.5/10**

**Verdict:** Must Watch. Milly Alcock is a star. Jason Momoa is a delight. The film is not perfect, but it's a bold, refreshing take on a character who has been overlooked for too long. Give it a chance. You might be surprised.
