## The Setup: A Regression Story With No Heart

*Tomb Raider King* is a webtoon about a man who is so good at his job that he can't be stopped — until he is, and then he goes back in time to be even better at his job. If that sounds like every other regression power fantasy, that's because it is.

The protagonist, Jooheon Seo, is a tomb raider. He raids tombs. He steals ancient relics. He is the best at what he does. Then he is betrayed by the people he trusted, killed, and wakes up in the past with all his memories intact. He decides to use his future knowledge to claim every powerful relic before anyone else can, and to destroy the people who betrayed him.

It's a premise that has potential. The idea of a man who has already seen the end of the story, and is now rewriting it from the beginning, is inherently interesting. The webtoon could have explored the weight of that knowledge, the cost of his revenge, the relationships he left behind.

Instead, it explores nothing. It's just a checklist of relics to acquire and enemies to defeat.

---

## The Plot: A Checklist of Convenience

The story is a sequence of events. Jooheon finds a relic. Someone else finds out about the relic. Jooheon defeats them. He moves on. He finds another relic. The cycle repeats.

There's no tension because Jooheon is never in danger. He's always one step ahead. He's always the most powerful person in the room. He knows exactly what to do and exactly how to do it. There's no struggle. There's no growth. There's just an endless procession of victories.

The villains are even worse. They're evil because the plot requires them to be evil. They don't have personalities. They don't have motivations. They just show up, threaten Jooheon, and get destroyed.

The supporting cast is similarly forgettable. There's a girl who likes him. There's a guy who's jealous of him. There's a mentor figure who teaches him things he already knows. None of them have any agency. None of them matter. They're just there to fill the space between action sequences.

---

## The Characters: A Hero With No Flaws

**Jooheon Seo** is not a character. He's a power fantasy. He's perfect. He's smart. He's strong. He's right about everything. He doesn't have flaws. He doesn't have doubts. He doesn't have emotions beyond a vague sense of satisfaction when his enemies are defeated.

He's not likable. He's not relatable. He's not interesting. He's just a guy who wins because the plot says he wins.

The supporting cast is even worse. They're not characters. They're cheerleaders. They exist to praise Jooheon, to be impressed by Jooheon, to be rescued by Jooheon. They don't have their own goals, their own dreams, their own struggles. They're just there to make Jooheon look good.

---

## The Writing: Exposition, Convenience, and Repetition

The writing in *Tomb Raider King* is not good. It relies heavily on exposition. Characters explain things to each other that they should already know. The narrator explains things that are obvious. The plot moves forward through conveniences — Jooheon just happens to be in the right place at the right time. He just happens to find the relic he needs. He just happens to have the power required to defeat his enemies.

The action sequences are well-drawn, but they're hollow. They're not about tension. They're not about strategy. They're just about showing how cool Jooheon is.

---

## The Verdict: A Power Fantasy That Forgets to Be Fun

*Tomb Raider King* is not a terrible webtoon. It's just a forgettable one. It's a story that has all the ingredients for a compelling narrative — a betrayed hero, a second chance, a world of ancient magic — and does nothing with them. It's a checklist of tropes executed without any imagination.

If you're looking for a mindless power fantasy to pass the time, it might work for you. But if you're looking for a story with emotional depth, interesting characters, or meaningful stakes, you'll be disappointed.

**Score: 2.5/5**

**Verdict:** Skip. Read *Solo Leveling* instead. It's not a masterpiece, but at least it knows how to make a power fantasy entertaining. *Tomb Raider King* is just a tomb full of wasted potential.
