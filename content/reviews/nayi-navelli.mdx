## The Setup: A Haveli, A Curse, And A Lot of Confusion

There is a very specific charm to Indian horror-comedies. When done right, they can be an absolute blast. But when a film struggles to find the right balance between genuine scares and over-the-top comedy, it ends up feeling like a mess. *Nayi Navelli* falls somewhere in the middle.

Directed by Balaji Mohan, the film follows a young woman who inherits a sprawling, ancestral haveli. But the house comes with a catch: a dark history and a supposed curse that has kept the family away for generations. She decides to move in, determined to fix the place up, only to discover that the ghosts are very real—and they are not happy about the new renovations.

The film wants to be a spooky ride mixed with family drama and situational comedy. It has all the ingredients: a creepy mansion, a skeptical protagonist, quirky side characters, and a lot of screaming. But the final dish is a bit undercooked.

---

## The Plot: A Familiar Haunting

If you have seen a haunted house movie before, you know exactly how this goes. The protagonist arrives. Strange things happen. Doors creak. Shadows move. The locals warn her to leave. She ignores them. Then the ghosts show up.

*Nayi Navelli* follows this template to the letter. There are no real surprises. The comedy comes from the interactions between the protagonist and the eccentric villagers, but the jokes are hit-or-miss. The horror elements rely on cheap jump scares rather than building genuine dread.

The biggest issue is the pacing. The first half is fairly engaging as it sets up the mystery and the quirky world of the village. But the second half completely loses its way. It abandons the plot for a series of chaotic, poorly edited CGI action sequences that feel like they belong in a different movie.

---

## The Acting: Yami Gautam Carries the Film

**Yami Gautam Dhar** is the best thing about this film. She is a highly capable actress, and she brings a lot of energy and conviction to her role. She handles both the dramatic and comedic moments with ease, and she makes you root for her character even when the script is failing her.

**Mita Vashisht** and **Sunita Rajwar** are also excellent additions to the cast. They bring a sense of gravitas and genuine humor, elevating the material they are given. 

However, the rest of the supporting cast feels like they are in a completely different, much louder movie. The villains are cartoonish, and the comedic sidekicks are more annoying than funny.

---

## The Technical Side: Great Setups, Weak Payoffs

The production design is genuinely impressive. The haveli feels lived-in and rich with history. It's dark, dusty, and full of corners for things to hide in. The cinematography does a good job of capturing that eerie, rustic atmosphere.

But when the ghosts finally appear, the film falls apart. The visual effects are surprisingly poor for a modern theatrical release. The ghosts look like they were rendered for a B-grade TV show, which completely kills whatever tension the film had built up.

---

## The Verdict: A Forgettable Haunting

*Nayi Navelli* is not a terrible film. It has its moments. Yami Gautam is always watchable, and there are a few laughs scattered throughout. But it is not a memorable film either. It's too long, the VFX are weak, and it doesn't bring anything new to the horror-comedy genre.

If you are a fan of Yami Gautam or you just want a mindless weekend watch with a couple of jump scares, you might enjoy it. But if you are looking for a genuinely scary or deeply funny film, look elsewhere.

**Score: 5.5/10**

**Verdict:** One-Time Watch. For Yami Gautam's performance and the atmospheric first half. Don't expect much from the climax.
