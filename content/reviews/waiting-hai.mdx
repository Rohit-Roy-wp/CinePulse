## The Great Indian Waiting Game: A Scam Hiding in Plain Sight

The Indian Railways is the lifeline of the nation, but for millions of travellers, it is also a source of endless frustration. The dreaded "Waiting List" is a cultural phenomenon, a shared trauma that unites us in our desperation for a confirmed berth. *Waiting Hai* takes this universally relatable struggle and turns it into a gripping, humorous, and surprisingly poignant investigation into a scam that has been hiding in plain sight for decades[citation:1][citation:3].

Directed by National Award-winning filmmaker Sameer Vidwans, this MX Original series is not just about a crime; it is about the people caught on both sides of the law. It is a "shuddh desi" dramedy that understands the pulse of its audience, delivering a story that is as entertaining as it is eye-opening[citation:3][citation:7].

---

## Plot & The Tatkal Racket: A Cat-and-Mouse Chase

The series introduces us to **Sugam Mishra** (Divyenndu Sharma), a newly appointed Railway Protection Force (RPF) constable. He is a simple, pure-hearted man from a small town, recently married, and eager to do good in his new job[citation:4][citation:5]. His world is turned upside down when he notices something deeply wrong: while ordinary passengers like him are stuck on waitlists, certain agents seem to secure confirmed Tatkal tickets with alarming ease[citation:1][citation:3].

What begins as a simple curiosity soon spirals into a full-blown investigation. Sugam discovers a sophisticated racket involving a hacker named **Afroz Hamza** (Bhuvan Arora), a brilliant but underprivileged young man from Bihar who has found a way to bypass the IRCTC system[citation:1][citation:4]. The series sets up a classic cat-and-mouse chase between the determined constable and the elusive hacker, but it cleverly subverts expectations by making neither character purely black or white.

Sugam's pursuit is driven by his naive belief in doing the right thing, while Afroz's crimes stem from a complex place of neglect and a desire for recognition[citation:4]. Their parallel lives, running on opposite sides of the law, form the emotional core of the narrative[citation:1].

## Acting Masterclass: Divyenndu, Bhuvan Arora, and the Grey Area

**Divyenndu Sharma** delivers a performance that is a complete departure from his iconic Munna Tripathi from *Mirzapur*. As Sugam Mishra, he is earnest, a little naive, and utterly believable. Divyenndu brings a quiet dignity to the character, a man who is not the "brightest person in the room" but has the courage to follow his inner voice[citation:5]. His struggle against a bureaucratic system that seems rigged against the common man is both funny and frustratingly real[citation:4].

**Bhuvan Arora** is a revelation as Afroz Hamza. Following his performance in *Farzi*, he once again embodies a morally grey tech genius, but Afroz is a different beast altogether. He is introverted, often sitting alone, and carries the weight of a lifetime of being ignored[citation:4]. Arora's portrayal makes you understand, and even sympathize with, the hacker, even as you root for Sugam to catch him. As Arora himself noted, "We are all grey in life"[citation:4].

The supporting cast, including **Harshita Gaur** as Sugam's supportive wife Princy and veteran actors like **Kumud Mishra**, adds depth to the world of the series. Harshita and Divyenndu, reuniting after *Mirzapur*, share a warm, domestic chemistry that grounds the story in a relatable reality[citation:2][citation:9].

## The CinePulse Verdict

*Waiting Hai* is a triumph of "desi" storytelling. It takes a mundane, everyday frustration and turns it into a compelling, character-driven drama. The show's greatest strength is its empathy, its refusal to paint its characters with a broad brush, and its ability to find humor in the absurdity of the system. It is a story about ordinary people, as the creators intended, and that is exactly what makes it extraordinary[citation:1][citation:5].

A solid **4.0 out of 5 stars**. A highly recommended watch for anyone who has ever cursed their luck at a railway ticket counter.
