## The Setup: A Great Idea Buried Under Three Hours of Noise

There is a genuinely important story somewhere inside *Peddi*. In 1996, roughly 18,000 villages across India existed without official names on any government record. The people living there had no voter cards, no addresses, no recognition from the state. They existed without existing — at least on paper [citation:13]. That is a premise worthy of a great film.

*Peddi* is not that film.

Director Buchi Babu Sana, following up his acclaimed debut *Uppena*, has assembled all the right ingredients. Ram Charan in the lead. A.R. Rahman on music. Rathnavelu on cinematography. A supporting cast featuring Shiva Rajkumar, Jagapathi Babu, and Boman Irani. The ambition is undeniable. The execution is a disaster.

What we get instead is a 189-minute endurance test that wants to be *Rangasthalam*, *Dangal*, and a social justice drama all at once — and succeeds at none of them.

---

## The Plot: A Checklist of Everything Wrong with Mass Cinema

Peddi (Ram Charan) is a daily wage laborer in a remote, unnamed village in Andhra Pradesh. He works in a jaggery factory. He's also a gifted cricketer who hires himself out to local teams for money. When he falls for Achiyamma (Janhvi Kapoor), the daughter of a local politician, he gets on the wrong side of Rambujji (Divyenndu), the rich man's son who also wants her. After a violent confrontation, Peddi is forced to leave cricket and train in wrestling under Gournaidu (Shiva Rajkumar). His goal? Win an Olympic medal and force the government to recognize his village [citation:10][citation:13].

That summary sounds coherent. The film is not.

The first hour is spent on the romance track — and calling it a "romance" is generous. Peddi sees Achiyamma. He decides he wants her. He tells his friends that since she's from a higher social standing and would never be with him anyway, he'll just touch her. If something goes wrong, "you only live once" [citation:4]. He then climbs over her wall, enters her private space, and touches her without consent. The film frames this as an "accidental kiss" and moves on as if nothing happened [citation:4].

That is not romance. That is assault. And the film presents it as charming.

The sports sequences — the cricket matches and wrestling training — are the only parts where the film briefly comes alive. Ram Charan is convincing as an athlete, and the matches have some energy. But even here, the film undercuts itself. Peddi is so effortlessly great at everything — cricket, wrestling, sprinting — that there is no tension, no struggle, no reason to root for him. He doesn't earn his victories. He just has them [citation:1].

## The Characters: Ram Charan Deserves Better

**Ram Charan** is the sole reason this film is watchable. He has physically transformed himself, and he carries the weight of the role with genuine commitment. His performance in the hospital sequence in the second half — where he confronts the physical toll of his choices — is genuinely powerful [citation:13]. It's a reminder that he is one of the finest actors of his generation when given material worthy of him. This material is not worthy.

**Janhvi Kapoor** is given nothing to do. Her introduction scene involves removing her dupatta and standing there while the camera lingers on her body for three minutes [citation:4]. She exists to be looked at, to be "won," and to disappear when the plot no longer needs her. She deserves better. So does the audience.

**Jagapathi Babu** as Appalasoori, the village elder who has spent thirty years fighting for recognition, brings real dignity to the role. He's the emotional anchor the film needed more of [citation:13].

**Shiva Rajkumar** as Gournaidu, the wrestling coach, makes his presence felt. But like everyone else, he's underutilized.

**Divyenndu** as Rambujji is the villain for the first half, then disappears, then shows up in the pre-climax as a changed man for no reason. It's laughable [citation:10].

## The Message: Social Justice for Show, Not for Substance

*Peddi* wants you to believe it's a film about marginalized communities fighting for dignity. The Indian Express called it "a well-intentioned step forward in marginalised storytelling" that "ultimately undermines its own message through misogyny, an overpowered protagonist, and a hollow understanding of social justice" [citation:1].

That assessment is accurate. The film depicts poverty, ostracism, and the struggle for recognition — but it does so through fictional labels that keep it safely neutral. It never engages with caste in concrete terms. It reduces systemic oppression to a backdrop for one man's superhero journey [citation:1].

And then there's the "labour as training" arc. Peddi's grueling physical labor — already a marker of his social identity — is reframed as athletic training. The upper-caste wrestlers he competes against undergo no such process. The film doesn't critique this inequality. It normalizes it [citation:1].

This is not a film about social justice. It's a film that uses the *aesthetic* of social justice to make its hero look noble.

---

## The Technical Side: The Only Things That Work

A.R. Rahman's score is the film's most consistent achievement. The background score does real heavy lifting, pulling scenes toward emotional territory the writing never earns [citation:13]. There are moments in the second half where Rahman's music makes you feel something the film hasn't earned.

Rathnavelu's cinematography is excellent. The rural landscapes, the sports sequences, the 1990s setting — all shot with confidence and texture. The film looks better than it deserves to [citation:13].

---

## The Verdict: A Waste of Talent, Time, and a Good Premise

*Peddi* is not just a bad film. It's a frustrating one. Because buried underneath the misogyny, the weak writing, and the three-hour runtime, there is a story worth telling. There is a performance worth watching. There is a technical team operating at the top of their game.

But none of that matters if the foundation is rotten.

**Score: 4.0/10**

**Verdict:** Skip. Watch *Rangasthalam* instead if you want Ram Charan at his best. Watch *Sarpatta Parambarai* if you want a sports drama with real stakes. Watch anything else. *Peddi* is a film that had everything except a reason to exist.
