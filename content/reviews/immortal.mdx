## The Setup: A Roommate From Hell (Literally)

The premise is simple, effective, and ripe with potential. Magi (GV Prakash Kumar) is a struggling young man in Chennai, desperate for a place to stay. Out of nowhere, a beautiful woman named Sindhu (Kayadu Lohar) offers him a room in her luxurious apartment for a rent that's too good to be true. He ignores every red flag — and there are many — because he's blinded by her beauty and the prospect of sharing a home with a woman[citation:4][citation:13].

By the time he realises that Sindhu is not who she claims to be — that she's a thousand-year-old creature with supernatural powers and a very specific plan for him — it's too late. He's trapped[citation:2].

It's a classic contained thriller setup. One location. Two characters. A cat-and-mouse dynamic where the mouse doesn't even know he's in a trap until the door slams shut. Films like *Pizza* and *Black* have mined similar territory with great success. *Immortal* wants to join their ranks. But it doesn't.

---

## The Plot: A Mythology That Never Takes Shape

The film opens with a prologue set a thousand years ago, in a tribal village where a child is born something monstrous[citation:2]. That's the extent of the mythology. The film hints at something ancient and dark, but it never bothers to explain what Sindhu actually is.

Is she a vampire? She has fangs, she drinks blood, she's immortal. But she also has powers that don't fit the vampire template — she can read minds, manifest objects, and create elaborate illusions[citation:7][citation:11]. The film treats these abilities as convenient plot devices rather than coherent rules of a supernatural world.

The result is a creature that feels undefined. Critics noted this as a fundamental flaw: "the creature doesn't fit into either category, it doesn't have proper characteristics of either"[citation:4]. Without clear rules, there are no real stakes. Sindhu can do anything the script needs her to do — and when she can't, it's because the plot requires her not to.

## The Acting: Kayadu Lohar Carries the Film

**Kayadu Lohar** is the film's greatest asset. Her Sindhu is charming, alluring, and genuinely unsettling. She has to walk a tightrope between warmth and menace, and she does it with a confidence that suggests she understands the assignment better than the director does. Her performance is the reason the first half works at all[citation:2][citation:13].

**GV Prakash Kumar** is fine as Magi. He's playing an ordinary man caught in extraordinary circumstances, and he conveys fear and vulnerability effectively. But the character is so thinly written — a "horny young man" with no other defining traits[citation:7] — that there's only so much an actor can do.

The supporting cast is forgettable. Lollu Sabha Maaran's comedic relief falls flat, and the film's attempts at humour feel like they belong in a different movie.

---

## The Technical Side: The VFX Is the Star

For a Tamil fantasy film, *Immortal* punches above its weight in the visual effects department. The creature design is convincing, and the climax features some genuinely impressive CG work that rivals bigger-budget productions[citation:1][citation:10]. Sam CS's background score is also a highlight, creating atmosphere and tension even when the screenplay doesn't[citation:4].

But technical competence can't save a film with no emotional core. The romance between Magi and Sindhu is superficial — we're told they're drawn to each other, but we never feel it. The survival stakes are hollow because we don't care enough about Magi to fear for his life.

---

## The Verdict: A Missed Opportunity

*Immortal* has all the ingredients for a compelling contained thriller: a charismatic antagonist, a claustrophobic setting, and a premise that promises psychological horror. But it squanders them on a half-baked mythology and a screenplay that prioritises cheap sensuality over genuine suspense.

It's not the worst film ever made. It's not even the worst vampire film. It's just... forgettable. Ironically, for a film titled *Immortal*, there's very little about it that stays with you.

**Score: 5.0/10**

**Verdict:** Skip. If you're curious about Tamil cinema's attempt at a vampire story, wait for streaming. The VFX and Kayadu Lohar's performance are worth a look, but the film around them isn't. Watch *Pizza* instead for a better executed trapped-in-a-room thriller.
