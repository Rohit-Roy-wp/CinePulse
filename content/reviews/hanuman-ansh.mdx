## The Setup: A Chosen One, A Divine Destiny

There's a long tradition in Indian cinema of blending mythology with modern storytelling. *Hanuman* (2005), *Jai Shri Krishna*, *Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama* — these are films that understood the power of these ancient stories. They treated the source material with reverence, but they also understood the importance of making it relevant to a modern audience.

*Hanuman Ansh* — which translates to "The Essence of Hanuman" — wants to join that tradition. It wants to be a film about devotion, destiny, and the power of faith. It wants to be a film about a young boy who discovers that he is more than he thought he was. It wants to be an epic.

What it actually is, unfortunately, is a mediocre fantasy film that mistakes spectacle for substance.

---

## The Plot: A Template So Familiar It Feels Pre-Used

The story follows Aryan (Dev Joshi), a young boy who is special. He doesn't know it, of course. He's just a normal kid — until he starts exhibiting strange powers. Super strength. Visions. The ability to sense evil. His grandmother (Shubhangi Atre) knows the truth: he is the reincarnation of a portion of Lord Hanuman's divine energy, born to fight the demonic forces that have been gathering for centuries.

Enter the villain: a powerful sorcerer named Maha Asur (Rajesh Khattar), who wants to use Aryan's powers to unleash a reign of darkness. And enter the mentor: a wise sage (Vishal Karwal) who teaches Aryan how to control his abilities and embrace his destiny.

If you've seen *Harry Potter*, *The Matrix*, or any superhero origin story, you've seen this film's plot. It's the Hero's Journey, stripped of any complexity, with Hindu mythology grafted onto it as window dressing. The film doesn't try to do anything new. It doesn't try to subvert expectations. It just follows the template, hitting every beat with mechanical precision.

---

## The Acting: Dev Joshi Carries It, But He Carries It Alone

**Dev Joshi** — known for his role in the television series *Baalveer* — is the film's biggest asset. He brings a genuine innocence to the role of Aryan. He's wide-eyed, earnest, and completely convincing as a young boy who is trying to understand his place in the world. There's a sincerity in his performance that almost makes you believe in the story he's telling.

But he's alone. The supporting cast is largely forgettable. **Rajesh Khattar** as Maha Asur is the standard issue villain — he laughs menacingly, he gives long speeches, he threatens the hero. He's not a character. He's a plot device. **Anushka Sen** is introduced as a love interest and then promptly forgotten for most of the film. And **Shubhangi Atre** as the grandmother is given little to do except provide exposition and look worried.

---

## The Technical Side: Ambitious But Uneven

For a film with a budget of around ₹30 crore, *Hanuman Ansh* is surprisingly ambitious. The visual effects are not always good, but they're trying. The aerial sequences — where Aryan flies through the clouds, or battles demons in the sky — are well-staged. The creature designs are interesting, even if the execution is sometimes lacking.

The background score is rousing, and it does a lot of work to sell the emotion of the scene. The cinematography is competent, though it relies heavily on the standard "orange and blue" colour palette that has become so common in modern fantasy films.

But the technical ambition only highlights the film's weaknesses. When the story is thin, and the characters are flat, the spectacle starts to feel hollow. There's no weight to the action because there are no stakes.

---

## The Verdict: A Missed Opportunity

*Hanuman Ansh* is not a bad film. It's not a good film either. It's a film that exists somewhere in the middle — a film that had the potential to be something special but was held back by a weak script and a lack of focus.

It wants to be a mythological epic. It wants to be a coming-of-age story. It wants to be a superhero origin story. It ends up being none of these things convincingly.

**Score: 6.0/10**

**Verdict:** One-Time Watch. If you're a fan of mythological fantasy films, you might find something to enjoy. If you're looking for a film that does something new with the genre, look elsewhere. *Hanuman Ansh* is a film that plays it safe, and in doing so, it forgets to be interesting.
