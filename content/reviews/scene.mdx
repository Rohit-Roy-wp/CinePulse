## The Setup: The Anti-Singam Cop

There's a moment early in *Scene* where Suriya's character, DSP Inbaraj, breaks the fourth wall and asks, "Who is that Suriya? Is he some big shot?" On paper, it sounds like the kind of meta-humour we've come to expect from modern star vehicles. But in the hands of director Jithu Madhavan, it's something else entirely. It's a declaration of intent. The superstar is not here to be worshipped. He's here to serve the story.

*Scene* is not your typical Suriya film. It's not *Singam*. It's not *Kaakha Kaakha*. It's a dark, quirky, and often hilarious crime comedy that treats its leading man as just another flawed, eccentric character in a world full of them. And it works beautifully.

---

## The Plot: A Killer, A Cop, and a Very Unusual Investigation

The film opens with Eeswaran (Naslen), a stone-cold serial killer who approaches murder with the procedural indifference of a businessman. He's methodical, calm, and utterly terrifying. Meanwhile, we meet DSP Inbaraj (Suriya), a cop who is so good at his job that he's bored of it. He wants to resign, but his superior, Stephen (Lal), won't let him go.

Inbaraj is brilliant but eccentric. He wears the same home clothes to the office, smokes like a chimney, and is so lazy that he often doesn't even enter crime scenes, instead asking his subordinates to describe everything to him. But there's a secret that explains his behavior — a weakness that makes him vulnerable even as he hunts down a dangerous killer.

What follows is a cat-and-mouse game between Inbaraj and Eeswaran that is as much a battle of wits as it is a clash of personalities. The film takes its time establishing this world, and while the first half has its slower moments, the second half picks up the pace with intense confrontations and a couple of genuinely surprising cameos.

---

## The Acting: Suriya Lets Go, Naslen Steals the Show

**Suriya** is the reason this film exists, and he knows it. But instead of coasting on his star power, he throws himself into the role of Inbaraj with a level of commitment that is genuinely refreshing. His comic timing is impeccable. His body language is quirky and unpredictable. And his willingness to be the butt of the joke — to let his character be flawed, lazy, and occasionally pathetic — is what makes Inbaraj so endearing.

**Naslen** as Eeswaran is a major surprise. Known for his boy-next-door roles in Malayalam cinema, he makes a remarkable transition here as a chilling, controlled antagonist. His calm, menacing presence is a perfect counterpoint to Suriya's chaotic energy. He holds his own against a superstar, which is no small feat.

**Nazriya Nazim** returns to Tamil cinema after 12 years, and she makes the most of it. Her character, Maya, is not a typical heroine. She's Inbaraj's house help, but she also has hidden abilities and her own action moments. She's an integral part of the team, not just a love interest.

---

## The Direction: Jithu Madhavan's Signature Chaos

Jithu Madhavan, the director of *Romancham* and *Aavesham*, brings his signature style to *Scene*. His films are populated with oddball characters who find themselves in extraordinary situations, and *Scene* is no exception. The humor is dark, the characters are weird, and the world feels lived-in and unpredictable.

The film's biggest strength is its subversion of expectations. It takes the familiar serial killer thriller template and gives it a makeover. The investigative portions are done in a light-hearted manner, filled with humor and smarts. Even the hero's "team" is a ragtag group of misfits, including his amnesiac forensic investigator father (Anandraj) and a perennially drunk criminal psychologist mother.

Sushin Shyam's music is another highlight. It's breezy, groovy, and never overbearing, perfectly complementing the film's dark, eccentric tone.

---

## The Verdict: A Worthy Experiment

*Scene* is not a perfect film. It takes some time to settle into its unique rhythm. The tonal shifts can be jarring. And some of the supporting characters feel underdeveloped. But it's a film that takes risks, and for the most part, those risks pay off.

It's a star vehicle that doesn't feel like a star vehicle. It's a thriller that makes you laugh. It's a Suriya film where Suriya is not the biggest attraction — the story is.

**Score: 7.5/10**

**Verdict:** Must Watch. If you're tired of formulaic mass hero films, *Scene* is a breath of fresh air. Suriya proves he's willing to experiment, and Naslen proves he's a talent to watch. Don't expect another *Aavesham*. Expect something new.
