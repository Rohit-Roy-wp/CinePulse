## The Setup: The Epic You've Been Waiting For

There are few stories in human history as enduring as the *Ramayana*. It has been told and retold for thousands of years — through oral tradition, through poetry, through theatre, through television. And now, it's being told through the most expensive Indian film ever made.

*Ramayana: Part 1* is the first chapter of Nitesh Tiwari's two-part adaptation of the epic. It's a film that was always going to be scrutinised. It's a film that was always going to be compared to the beloved Doordarshan series, to the countless retellings, to the expectations of millions. And it's a film that, against all odds, mostly succeeds.

It's not perfect. The CGI is uneven. The pacing drags in the first half. The ending is a cliffhanger that will frustrate those looking for a complete story. But it's a film that understands the weight of the story it's telling. It treats the source material with reverence, but it also makes it feel alive. It makes it feel new.

---

## The Plot: From Ayodhya to Lanka

The film follows the early chapters of the Ramayana. Rama (Ranbir Kapoor) is the beloved prince of Ayodhya, the son of King Dasharatha (Sheeba Chaddha). He is exiled for fourteen years by his stepmother, and his wife Sita (Sai Pallavi) and brother Lakshmana (Sunny Deol) insist on accompanying him. They live a simple life in the forest, far from the politics and intrigue of the court.

Meanwhile, in Lanka, the demon king Ravana (Yash) is plotting. He wants revenge on Rama and Lakshmana for the humiliation of his sister, Shurpanakha. He sends his brother Maricha to lure Rama away from Sita. And then he abducts Sita, taking her to Lanka.

What follows is the beginning of Rama's journey to rescue Sita. He forms an alliance with the monkey king Sugriva (a surprisingly good cameo by a major South Indian star). He meets Hanuman (a scene-stealing performance by a newcomer). And he sets his sights on Lanka.

The film's first half is slow. It takes its time establishing the world of Ayodhya, the bond between Rama and Sita, the loyalty of Lakshmana. It's beautiful to look at, but it's not propulsive. The second half is where the film kicks into gear — the abduction, the grief, the alliance, the march to war. It's here that the film's scale becomes truly epic.

## The Acting: Ranbir, Sai, and Yash

**Ranbir Kapoor** is a surprising choice for Rama. He's known for playing complex, morally ambiguous characters — not for playing the epitome of virtue. But he brings a quiet, gentle nobility to the role that is deeply moving. He doesn't play Rama as a god. He plays him as a man — a man who is struggling with grief, with exile, with the loss of his wife. It's a performance that earns its moments of grandeur.

**Sai Pallavi** as Sita is a revelation. She's fierce, graceful, and emotionally devastating. She's not just the damsel in distress. She's a woman of strength and conviction. Her scenes with Ranbir are quiet and beautiful. Her scenes with Yash are tense and terrifying. She's the heart of the film.

**Yash** as Ravana is the film's best asset. He's charismatic, terrifying, and tragic. He's not a cackling villain. He's a king, a scholar, a devotee of Shiva. He's also a man who has been corrupted by his own power. Yash plays him with a mix of arrogance and vulnerability that makes him unforgettable.

---

## The Technical Side: Ambitious But Uneven

The film's visual scale is genuinely breathtaking. The world-building is detailed and immersive. The production design is stunning. The costumes are beautiful. A.R. Rahman and Hans Zimmer's score is majestic and unforgettable. It elevates every scene it touches.

But the CGI is a mixed bag. Some of it is excellent — the creatures, the landscapes, the aerial shots. Some of it is not. The battle scenes, in particular, occasionally look unfinished. It's not enough to ruin the film, but it's enough to notice.

---

## The Verdict: A Worthy First Chapter

*Ramayana: Part 1* is not a perfect film. But it's a bold, ambitious, and deeply sincere film. It's a film that understands the weight of its story. It's a film that respects its audience. And it's a film that sets up a second chapter that promises to be even bigger.

**Score: 7.0/10**

**Verdict:** Must Watch. If you're a fan of mythology, you'll love it. If you're a fan of Indian cinema, you'll be proud of it. If you're looking for a complete story, wait for Part 2. But if you're looking for an epic that will transport you to another world, this is it.
